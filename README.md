
# 🌱 EcoFood — Stop Food Waste

## 📌 Project Description

EcoFood is a food-sharing project that helps reduce food waste and support people who need food.

Many families, restaurants, and supermarkets throw away food that is still safe to eat. At the same time, some people do not have enough food.

EcoFood proposes a mobile app or website where people and businesses can share extra food instead of throwing it away.

**Our slogan: Less waste. More food. A better tomorrow.**

---

## 🚨 1. The Problem

Food waste is a serious problem in many communities.

- People throw away food every day.
- Food waste creates environmental problems.
- Families lose money when they waste food.
- Some people do not have enough food to eat.

We need a simple solution to connect extra food with people who need it.

## 🎯 2. Main Objective

The main objective is to reduce food waste and help people who need food by creating a simple food-sharing system.

### Specific Objectives

- Connect families, restaurants, and supermarkets with people who need food.
- Share extra food before it is thrown away.
- Teach people about responsible food consumption.
- Measure the amount of food shared during the first month.

---

## 👥 3. Target Audience

EcoFood is designed for:

- Families
- Students
- Restaurants
- Supermarkets
- Volunteers
- People with limited resources

The project will start in one neighborhood and may expand to other communities in the future.

---

## 💡 4. Proposed Solution

We will create a mobile app or website where people can publish information about extra food.

Users will be able to:

- Publish available food.
- See food donations in their area.
- Contact people who offer food.
- Arrange a time and place to collect it.
- Help reduce food waste in their community.

Restaurants and supermarkets can also donate extra food that is safe to eat.

---

## 🧰 5. Materials and Resources

| Resource | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Mobile app or website | Show available food and donation information. |
| Volunteers | Organize donations and help users. |
| Restaurants and supermarkets | Provide extra food. |
| Food containers and bags | Transport food safely. |
| Social media and posters | Promote the project. |

---

## ⚙️ 6. How It Works

1. A family or restaurant has extra food.
2. The donor publishes the food on the EcoFood platform.
3. A person who needs food sees the offer.
4. Both people agree on a collection time and place.
5. The food is shared instead of wasted.

**Important:** All donated food must be safe to eat and stored correctly.

---

| Week | Activities | Expected Result |

| Week 2 | Design the platform and contact partners. | Prepare the first system. |
| Week 3 | Start sharing food in one neighborhood. | Make the first donations. |
| Week 4 | Collect feedback and review results. | Improve the project. |

# 📊 TAM, SAM, and SOM Analysis for EcoFood

## 1. Introduction

TAM, SAM, and SOM are important tools in business. They help companies understand their market, identify potential customers, and set realistic goals.

These concepts help EcoFood understand how many people and businesses could use its food-sharing platform.

- **TAM:** Total Addressable Market
- **SAM:** Serviceable Available Market
- **SOM:** Serviceable Obtainable Market

TAM represents the total market opportunity, SAM represents the market EcoFood can serve, and SOM represents the market EcoFood can realistically reach first.

---

## 2. TAM — Total Addressable Market

### Definition

TAM is the total market opportunity for a product or service if the business could reach every possible customer.

It represents the maximum potential size of the market.

### Application to EcoFood

For EcoFood, TAM includes all people and businesses that could benefit from a food-sharing platform.

These include:

- Families with extra food.
- Restaurants with extra food.
- Supermarkets and food shops.
- People who need affordable or free food.
- Organizations that support local communities.

In the future, EcoFood could expand to different cities and countries.

**Main question:** How many people and businesses could benefit from EcoFood in total?

---

## 3. SAM — Serviceable Available Market

### Definition

SAM is the part of TAM that a business can serve with its current business model, location, and resources.

It is smaller than TAM because a new business cannot serve every potential customer.

### Application to EcoFood

At the beginning, EcoFood will focus on Quito, Ecuador.

Its target market will include:

- Restaurants in Quito.
- Local supermarkets and food shops.
- Families with extra food.
- Community organizations.
- People who want to receive food donations.

EcoFood will start in one neighborhood before expanding to other areas.

**Main question:** Which customers can EcoFood serve with its current resources?

---

## 4. SOM — Serviceable Obtainable Market

### Definition

SOM is the part of SAM that a business can realistically reach in the short term.

It depends on the budget, team, marketing, competition, and available resources.

### Application to EcoFood

During its first year, EcoFood will focus on a small pilot project in one neighborhood in Quito.

Its first goals could be:

- Work with 10 local restaurants or food shops.
- Find 100 people or families who want to participate.
- Build a small group of volunteers.
- Organize safe food collection.
- Measure the amount of food shared.

These numbers are initial goals, not confirmed customers. EcoFood must contact potential partners and users to check their interest.

**Main question:** How many customers and partners can EcoFood realistically reach first?

---

## 5. Comparison: TAM vs. SAM vs. SOM

| Concept | Meaning | EcoFood Example |
|---|---|---|
| TAM | The total possible market | People and businesses that could benefit across different cities and countries. |
| SAM | The market EcoFood can serve | Potential users and food donors in Quito. |
| SOM | The market EcoFood can reach first | The first restaurants, shops, families, and users in one neighborhood. |

### Simple Explanation

- **TAM = The big opportunity.**
- **SAM = The market we can serve.**
- **SOM = The market we can reach first.**

---

## 6. Example of Market Size for EcoFood

The following example uses hypothetical numbers to explain how TAM, SAM, and SOM work.

These numbers are not official market statistics or confirmed EcoFood customers.

For this example, we will use restaurants and food shops as potential business partners.

### Step 1: Calculate TAM

Imagine that EcoFood could eventually work with 5,000 suitable restaurants and food shops across its planned expansion areas.

If each business paid $120 per year for optional premium services:

**Formula:**

`TAM = Potential Businesses × Annual Revenue per Business`

`TAM = 5,000 × $120`

**TAM = $600,000 per year**

This represents a hypothetical maximum annual revenue opportunity from these business customers.

### Step 2: Calculate SAM

At first, EcoFood plans to operate in Quito.

Imagine that 500 suitable restaurants and food shops in its target areas could use the service.

If each business paid $120 per year:

`SAM = 500 × $120`

**SAM = $60,000 per year**

This represents the hypothetical annual revenue opportunity in the initial service area.

### Step 3: Calculate SOM

During the first year, EcoFood aims to attract 25 paying business partners.

If each business paid $120 per year:

`SOM = 25 × $120`

**SOM = $3,000 per year**

This represents the example of annual revenue EcoFood could obtain from those 25 paying partners.

### Summary of the Example

| Market | Potential Paying Businesses | Annual Revenue |
|---|---:|---:|
| TAM | 5,000 | $600,000 |
| SAM | 500 | $60,000 |
| SOM | 25 | $3,000 |

**Important:** This example assumes that EcoFood charges for optional business services. The basic food-sharing service could be free. Real estimates require market research and evidence that businesses are willing to pay.
